Operating plan · Nigeria

Before you build

The licence you don't need and the one thing that would force you to get one, which gateway to sit on, what to register, what to charge, and sixteen names. Researched in August 2026 — I am not your lawyer or your accountant, and I say so where it matters.

01 — THE BIG ONE

Your architecture is the reason you're legal

I went looking for what licence a ticketing platform needs in Nigeria and found something that changes how you should think about the product.

The CBN licence that covers payment processing and merchant aggregation is the PSSP. To apply you need ₦100 million in share capital deposited with the CBN. A switching licence is ₦2 billion. Both are entirely out of reach, and neither is something you grow into quietly.

You don't need one — as long as you never touch the money. You operate as a merchant on Paystack's licence, using their split-payment feature. Paystack is the licensed party. You're a customer of theirs with a clever integration.

The line you must never cross

The moment you hold organisers' money — a wallet, a balance, an escrow, “we'll pay you out after the event” — you are doing something that needs a licence you cannot afford. That's not a technicality. It's the difference between a business and a criminal problem.

Every instinct will push you toward holding funds. It solves chargebacks, it solves fraud, it earns interest, and every competitor does it. Don't. Not until you have a lawyer and a licence.

So the no-custody design isn't just a nice line on the landing page. It's the only shape this business can legally take at your size — and it was in the code before either of us understood why it mattered.

Confirm this with a real lawyer

I'm reading public summaries, not advising you. Spend ₦150,000–₦300,000 with a Nigerian fintech lawyer for one written opinion on whether your split-payment model needs a licence. That is the single best money you'll spend this year, and it's cheap next to being wrong.

Two things about Paystack you should know now

Splits are limited to two accounts. Fine today — you and the organiser. But the day you want to pay a promoter a cut, or split between two co-hosts, Paystack can't do it and Monnify can. Don't design features that assume more than two parties.

Nigeria pays by transfer, not by card. Card fees are percentage-based; transfers are flat and much cheaper. Monnify's virtual accounts are among the cheapest in the market. If most of your buyers pay by transfer, moving to Monnify could cut your organisers' costs meaningfully — which is a selling point, not just plumbing.

Recommendation

Launch on Paystack. Watch what share of your buyers pay by transfer for the first few months. If it's most of them, add Monnify.

The code already supports this: payments sit behind an interface, so a second gateway is one new file and an environment variable, not a rewrite. That abstraction was built on principle and is about to earn its keep.

03 — REGISTRATION

What you have to set up

Paystack will let you test with a personal account, but to take real money as a business you need a Registered Business: a CAC certificate and a corporate bank account whose name matches it exactly.

Limited company, not Business Name

A Business Name is cheaper and it is the wrong choice here. It gives you no separation between the business and you personally. Given the chargeback exposure — money already paid out, a dispute months later — a Business Name means your own savings are on the line. A limited company is the entire point of a limited company.

Tax — you're almost certainly exempt for now

Small companies pay 0% company income tax. VAT registration doesn't bite until ₦50 million of turnover.

The quiet advantage nobody mentions

Your turnover is your fee income, not the value of tickets sold — because the ticket money never passes through you. Sell ₦400 million of tickets at ₦200 each and your turnover is ₦2 million, not ₦400 million.

A platform that held funds would have to argue about that. You don't. No-custody keeps you a small company on paper for far longer than your volume suggests.

Two I could not pin down

Data protection. You collect buyer names and emails, so the Nigeria Data Protection Act applies, and above certain volumes you must register with the NDPC. I couldn't confirm the current thresholds — ask the lawyer at the same time as the licence question.

The CIT threshold. Sources disagree on whether the small-company line is ₦25m or ₦100m after the 2025 reform. Your accountant will know. Either way you're well under it.

04 — PRICING

Settle the number

From the earlier note, restated as one decision. A flat ₦200 is roughly 10% of a ₦2,000 ticket and 0.13% of a ₦150,000 one, in a market where ticket prices span about 75×. One number cannot serve both ends.

You stay 3–4× cheaper than the incumbent everywhere, “a flat fee per ticket, never a percentage” is still literally true, and you roughly double revenue per ticket.

And let organisers pass it to the buyer

Tix already offers this, so it's expected rather than clever. A switch: absorb the fee, or add it to the buyer's total. When the buyer pays, the organiser's cost is zero and your cheap-ticket problem disappears completely.

My recommendation, in one line

Four bands, plus a pass-to-buyer switch that defaults to off. Default off matters: “the price you see is the price you pay” is worth more to your buyers than the fee is to your organisers.

05 — OPERATIONS

What one person can actually run

Everything above is arithmetic. This is the part that decides whether you last: there is one of you, and events happen at night, at weekends, all at once.

You cannot attend events

Twenty events on one Saturday is twenty doors you aren't at. So the door has to work without you — which is why the manual code entry matters more than it looks. It's not a fallback, it's the thing that stops a bad night becoming a refund.

Publish support hours, and make them real

“WhatsApp, 9am–11pm Friday and Saturday” is honest and achievable. Silence at 10pm on a Saturday is how you lose an organiser permanently. Promise less than you can do.

Hold new organisers to a limit

Someone can list a fake event, sell 400 tickets, take the money straight to their own bank and vanish — that's what instant settlement means. Cap what a new organiser can sell until they've run one event cleanly, and verify identity above a threshold. This is the fraud control you can afford; holding funds is the one you can't.

Write the refund policy before you need it

The organiser has the money, so only they can refund. Buyers won't see it that way — they bought from your site. Say so on the checkout page, in plain words, before anyone pays.

How to choose, in an afternoon

  1. Pick threeFrom the shortlisted five. Trust your gut — you'll be saying it for years.
  2. The noisy room testSay each one down a phone line to a friend, no spelling. If they can't repeat it back, it's out. Your buyers hear this name in clubs.
  3. Search CACCheck name availability on the CAC public search before anything else. If CAC says no, nothing else matters.
  4. Check the domainThe .com and the .ng. A .ng is entirely respectable here and often available when .com isn't.
  5. Search the name plus “Nigeria”Ten minutes on Google and Instagram. If an events brand already owns it locally, move on.
  6. Reserve it at CACName reservation is cheap and holds it while you register the company.
If you want my answer

Gate if you can get a usable domain — it's the product in one syllable and it grows with you. Jaiye if you want to be unmistakably Nigerian and own something no competitor can copy. Lanyard if you're serious about going after conferences, because it does the positioning for you.

07 — ORDER OF OPERATIONS

What to do, in what order

  1. Email Paystack about chargebacksOn a split transaction, who bears a dispute — you or the subaccount? Free, ten minutes, and it changes everything downstream.
  2. One hour with a fintech lawyerDoes this model need a licence? What does NDPA require of you? Get it in writing.
  3. Pick the nameCAC search, then domain, then reserve.
  4. Register the companyLimited, not Business Name. Then the corporate bank account.
  5. Activate Paystack as a Registered BusinessNeeds the CAC certificate and a matching corporate account.
  6. Decide the feeFour bands, pass-to-buyer off by default, unless the lawyer's answer changes the picture.
  7. Then buildBands, the pass-to-buyer switch, the rename. All small once the decisions are made.
Steps 1 and 2 cost almost nothing

An email and one meeting. Everything else waits on them, and doing them out of order is how people build for six months on a foundation that turns out to be illegal.
